## READING SEMANTIC PENS: INTENT, NOT LITERAL ORDERS

A semantic pen's marks say what the user wants, together with the rest of the sketch. They are not an order to paste the pen's constraint onto whatever it drove where it was taken from.

- A constraint pen carries a PATTERN and an ORIGIN. The pattern is what its computation does to a quantity: shrinks it by a ratio along an order, oscillates it, scatters it by noise, branches it recursively, eases it in time. The origin is the property it determined where it was copied from: a size, an angle, a spacing, a color. `source.label`, the constraint's label and description, and the pen's `meaning` tell the origin. The origin is a hint, not a param.
- Decide what each pen's pattern DRIVES from the sketch. Look at what visibly changes along the marks drawn with it (in the image and in the order and geometry of its strokes), what other marks it overlaps or is drawn on, and the instruction. When the drawing shows the pattern changing a different quantity than its origin, RE-TARGET it to that quantity.
- Marks of different pens that overlap, cross or are drawn on the same form are ONE idea: read them TOGETHER. Usually one gives the form or effect and the other modulates a quantity OF that effect along the order the marks show (how strong, how fast, how dense, how far it spreads). Do not read them as two independent effects that each act on their own origin's quantity.
- Check the reading against the drawing. The result must change exactly what the marks show changing. Never apply a pattern to a quantity whose change the sketch does not show, e.g. resizing items whose drawn size stays the same, or recoloring items drawn in one color.
- Keep what the computation IS: its rule and the meaning of its params. Adapt what it is applied to, its direction, range and param values to the sketch. Its constraint is a starting point, not something to paste as is. Adapt which node gets it and which property it determines there, which of its params matter there, their values, and how it combines with the other pens' marks, to what the marks show. Leave out params whose effect the sketch does not show.
- An explicit statement wins: when the instruction or a pen's `meaning` (the user may have written it) says what the pen should drive, follow it.
- When the sketch is ambiguous, choose the reading in which every pen contributes to ONE coherent visible change, and say that reading in plain words (in the node or constraint description, or in the prompt).
